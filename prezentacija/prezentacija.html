resample from norm(p − q)⁺

The two cases — accepted guess and post-rejection correction — sum to exactly p(x).

The result is exactly the target distribution, no matter how poor the draft is. A bad draft only means slower, never different.

Confirmed empirically: across 100,000 samples the deviation stays below sampling noise.

Model

Everything follows from two numbers

γtokens the draft guesses
αhow many the target accepts
cwhat the draft costs
Etokens per target call
speedup  =  E / (γc + 1)
The numerator grows with the draft's quality, the denominator with its cost. There is a speedup only while the gain outruns the price.

Analysis

α decays with depth

0.905γ = 1
0.822γ = 3
0.749γ = 5
0.655γ = 8

The draft almost always gets the first guess right. The further it runs without correction, the more it drifts from the target.

That is why efficiency falls from 0.97 to 0.47: theory says larger γ always helps, measurement disagrees.

Ceiling

A perfect draft buys only 15% more

α is not the bottleneck. The bottleneck is the cost of guessing and implementation overhead. So the payoff is in a cheaper draft, not a better one.
Distillation

Teaching the draft the target's distribution

  1. 01
    Data. The target generates continuations from wikitext train-split prefixes; its top-50 candidates are stored per position.
  2. 02
    Loss. The draft learns the whole distribution, not just the sampled token — because α measures how well distributions overlap.
  3. 03
    Result. α rises from 0.905 to 0.93: +6% at γ=3.
Since a perfect draft is worth at most ~15%, distillation captured roughly one third of the available headroom.

Comparison

Speedup against guess depth

All three curves peak at γ=3 and never cross. The gap widens with γ — the deeper you guess, the more a large draft costs you.
Conclusions

Five findings

  1. 01
    The method works. 2.75× at γ=3, with output identical to standard decoding.
  2. 02
    The theory holds. The speedup model reproduces measurements up to γ ≤ 3.
  3. 03
    Smaller drafts win. 0.5B beats both 1.5B and 3B — cost grows faster than quality.
  4. 04
    Distillation helps modestly, +6%. Even a perfect draft is worth at most ~15%.
  5. 05
    Next step: a cheaper draft, not a better one.
